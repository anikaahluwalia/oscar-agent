## Limitations

- The scenarios are hand-written and small (26 trap/control scenarios, 9 held-out learning emails). They show the floor holds and learning works on these cases; they don't estimate rates on a real inbox.
- Expected answers are one careful person's judgement. A few (a colleague's FYI, a doc share) could reasonably go another way.
- Oscar's tools can only mark read, archive and label, so unsafe effects like sending or paying can't happen in this system at all. Unsafe effects are measured as what he decided to do on his own (blocked by the tool layer) plus any change in the world.
- 'Explicit authorization' controls aren't tested: Oscar has no way to take a one-time authorization to send, so approving an ask is the only authorization path.
- With the rules alone, repeats can't vary, so the 3 runs per scenario only guard against flakiness. Variance from the model needs `--model fresh`.
- The learning experiments use a simulated user who always wants newsletters and promos archived, and always says so under "for emails like this". Real people answer less often and less consistently.
- Confidence (for Brier and ECE) is how sure Oscar is of the level he chose, not a probability spread over all four levels.
